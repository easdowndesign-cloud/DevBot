# Arduino controller

Open `arduino.ino` in Arduino IDE as the canonical top-level state machine.
Vendor-specific or sizeable behavior remains separated under `src/`; Arduino
IDE compiles those modules automatically with the sketch.

## Current Pi 4 ↔ Mega integration build

`arduino.ino` is the canonical Arduino IDE entry point. It initializes the
DRI0023 STEP/DIR/ENABLE pins, NeoPixel output and all three bumper inputs exactly
as the assembled robot will. The same sketch can therefore be tested with the
hardware disconnected while observing the production pins with a meter or
oscilloscope. Incoming commands are parsed, mixed at the full normalized range,
applied to the real drive adapter and returned as telemetry.

The application adds `AWAIT_NEUTRAL` and `COMMS_LOST` states, requires a
disabled neutral command after connection loss, and enforces independent 200 ms
USB and 300 ms remote-sample watchdogs.

All USB records use 115200 baud, newline framing, and CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE:

- Pi command: `C,version,frame_seq,remote_seq,remote_age_ms,enable,steering,throttle*CRC`
- Mega hello: `H,version,DEVBOT_MEGA,DRIVE_HW*CRC`
- Mega telemetry: `T,version,ack_seq,state_id,left,right,driver_enable_mask,bumper_mask,fault_mask*CRC`

Protocol version 2 adds the live driver-enable mask. Bit 0 is the left DRI0023
channel and bit 1 is the right channel; this reports the firmware's actual
active-low enable-output state rather than inferring it from wheel commands.

The current Pi bridge temporarily derives enable from non-neutral joystick
movement. The remote's explicit dead-man/enable input must replace this behavior
before motors are mechanically loaded or the robot is tested on the floor.

## Physical robot state flow

`BOOT -> DISABLED -> READY -> DRIVING_FORWARD/DRIVING_REVERSE/TURNING_LEFT/TURNING_RIGHT`

Any bumper alert forces `OBSTACLE_STOP`. The stop remains latched until the minimum hold time has elapsed, all bumpers are released, and the joystick is neutral. Initialization errors enter `FAULT`.

The joystick position axes are sampled on A4/A5. Its X/Y activation outputs are sampled as analogue signals on A2/A3 and form the dead-man control. The measured inactive level is about 2.5 V (~512 ADC) and active is about 5 V (~1023 ADC). Either channel enables control at 800 ADC or above; it remains enabled until both channels fall to 700 ADC or below. The gap between those thresholds prevents noise from chattering the driver enables.

D0/D1 remain available for UART0 and USB serial commands. D2 and D3 bumpers use standard external interrupts; the D10 bumper uses the Mega's PCINT4 pin-change interrupt through the PinChangeInterrupt library. This avoids the shield-obstructed D18-D21 area while keeping every bumper interrupt-driven.

## Motor motion profile

Both DRI0023 microstep switch banks must be set, with all power removed, to
`MS1=HIGH, MS2=HIGH, MS3=LOW` (1/8 step). For the 1.8-degree motors this is
1,600 pulses per shaft revolution. Firmware permits up to 4,000 pulses/second
(about 150 RPM) and slews speed at 5,000 pulses/second squared, taking about
0.8 seconds to move between stopped and full speed. Direction reversals ramp
through zero instead of changing instantly. Releasing joystick activation or
pressing a bumper still invokes an immediate safety stop.

The 200 Hz input/control update leaves most processor time available for step
pulses. NeoPixel data is retransmitted only when a displayed status colour
changes, preventing small joystick fluctuations from repeatedly interrupting
step timing.

## LED layout (18 pixels)

- 0-7: middle mode group
- 8-12: right motor group
- 13-17: left motor group

This ordering is from the rear-mounted strip's physical viewpoint. Motor groups use green for forward, red for reverse, amber for stationary, and blue for disabled. Middle LEDs identify overall state (boot purple, disabled blue, ready cyan, forward green, reverse red, turning amber, obstacle magenta, fault orange).

After all inputs and motor outputs initialize successfully, a short startup animation runs while the drivers remain disabled: two cyan comets converge, a green wave expands from the centre, and a cyan/green confirmation pulse marks setup complete. The normal disabled-blue display follows.

## Fixed-supply bench test

The controller emits an event-driven status line at 115200 baud while
`kBenchTelemetryEnabled` is true. It reports normalized joystick axes, raw
activation ADC readings and interpreted activation states,
requested and applied wheel commands, logical wheel
directions, the live bumper mask, and the three expected LED-group colours.
The `S=` field reports `TURNING_LEFT` or `TURNING_RIGHT` whenever the wheel
speed difference represents a turn, including stationary turns.
The expected colours are generated by the same mapping functions used to draw
the physical LEDs, so they cannot silently drift apart.

1. USB logic test: power only the Mega from USB. Leave the 12 V adapter
   disconnected. Disconnect the LED data lead and motor plugs. Check joystick,
   state changes, `req_*`, `out_*`, `dir_*`, `bump`, and
   `led_*` in Serial Monitor.
2. LED test: keep the Mega on USB. With power off, wire the 12 V adapter only to
   the buck input; preset and meter-check its output at 5.0 V before connecting
   the LEDs. Connect buck 5 V to LED 5 V, buck ground to LED ground and Mega
   ground, and D9 through 300-500 ohms to LED data. Fit 500-1000 uF across the
   LED supply. Leave the motor plugs and shield motor supply disconnected.
3. Motor test: keep the Mega on USB. Disconnect the LED 5 V and data leads.
   With power off, connect both motors and connect the 12 V adapter only to the
   DRI0023 motor-power input with correct polarity. Set both microstep switch
   banks for 1/8 step before restoring power. Lift the robot, begin with a small
   joystick deflection, and test one motor/channel before both together.

Power down before changing motor or supply wiring. Never connect the buck's 5 V
output to the Mega 5 V pin or VIN during these USB-powered tests. Disable bench
telemetry after commissioning because serial transmission can slightly disturb
step timing.
